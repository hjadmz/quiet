## Why I’m publishing this

I’m still early in my career, and that is exactly why I want a public technical archive.

I am building this site so I can show:

- what I learned,
- what I built,
- why I changed it,
- and what I ship next.

If the work is useful to me as a learning signal, it is useful to a future teammate or employer. I am not trying to pretend every decision was perfect. I am trying to show steady, repeatable judgment.

## FCA as the operating rule

I use **FCA** as my default sequence:

- **F** — Function first. If a feature does not help someone complete a task, it should not stay.
- **C** — Clarity second. If people have to guess what something means, it is not usable yet.
- **A** — Accountability always. I own what ships, and I explain the tradeoffs, not just the final screenshot.

For me, this is not a slogan. It is a practical filter.

## What “good” looks like for this site

This blog is a reading surface, so I optimize for:

- fast loads,
- stable layout on phones, tablets, and desktops,
- short paths to the next action,
- and no surprise complexity.

I keep visible UI controls minimal by default and avoid paid add-ons or novelty features that add friction. If a reader cannot access content with no special setup, it is not good enough.

## What I care about while I build

I care about:

- making structure legible over flashy effects,
- making links discoverable over decorative style,
- reducing edge-case friction,
- and publishing when the decision quality is honest, not just visually polished.

I also care about what breaks less often after launch than it does in local screenshots. A calm interface is not an excuse for fragile implementation.

## Why this helps hiring conversations

This archive is not a résumé in disguise. It is a map of how I work.

That means:

- I can make and revise decisions in public view.
- I can explain constraints without deflection.
- I can show a trajectory from intent to implementation.

If you are evaluating readiness, this helps more than a long list of tools on a profile page. It shows what I can own, not just what I can try.

## Next steps

The next posts will stay practical:

- short design studies,
- implementation walkthroughs,
- postmortems on what I broke,
- and how I fixed it.

I will keep adding only what improves reading and understanding.

Thank you for reading.
